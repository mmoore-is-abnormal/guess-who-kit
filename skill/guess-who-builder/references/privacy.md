# What stays out of the game

The test: would this person be uncomfortable seeing it on a shared screen in front of their team? The game is played live, so a fact that's fine in a Slack thread can feel exposing when it's the answer to a puzzle.

Leave out, even if the person posted it themselves:

- Health of any kind: conditions, injuries, medications, mental health, pregnancy, sleep problems.
- Family and relationships: spouses, partners, dating, engagements, weddings, kids (names, ages, circumstances), family hardship. Pets are fine.
- Religion, politics, union activity.
- Gender identity, sexual orientation, race, ethnicity, disability. Where someone is from is fine only if they chose it as their own fun fact ("grew up in Thailand").
- Legal names that differ from the name someone goes by. Always use the preferred name.
- People who are on leave, have left, or asked to sit out: remove them from the game entirely.
- Money: salary, debts, finances.
- Legal or criminal matters, immigration or citizenship, age or birth year.
- Exact home address or small-town names. Use the state or region; large cities are fine.
- Appearance-based jokes, anything embarrassing, anything that reads as a dig.
- Anything about work performance, and anything from DMs.
- Photos from posts (the game only uses Slack profile pictures).

Fine: hobbies, pets and pet names, food opinions, travel, sports, games, music, past jobs, talents, collections, a region or big city, harmless trivia they chose as their own "fun fact" (can wiggle their ears, never broken a bone).

When a sentence mixes both ("hang out with my dog and wife"), keep only the separable part ("hangs out with their dog"). When the sensitive part is the whole point, drop the fact.
